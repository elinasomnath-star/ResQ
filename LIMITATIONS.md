# Limitations

- The 16 zones are regular analytical grid cells clipped to Bantwal, not official village boundaries. Population is a modeled WorldPop 2025 constrained 1 km estimate: native raster pixels are summed by cell. The coarse raster can make small-zone estimates uneven or zero and does not resolve household-level population. The 2011 Census total (395,380) remains only in config as a labeled reference; the unadjusted raster sum (677,985.36) is 1.715× that value, and the difference is not reconciled.
- Sentinel-1 is used as candidate VH-change context for severity. The project does not establish a validated flood outline.
- Severity, affected population, and resource demand are estimates. Historical event reports and synthetic model augmentation do not provide observed demand for each grid cell.
- Relief-centre locations come from cached public map or event-report sources. Facility stock, capacity, and operational status are simulated or unknown.
- The road graph is from OpenStreetMap. Some closure matches are broad highway corridors rather than exact flood points; a routed path does not establish that a road was passable during an event.
- Module 7 covers all 16 Bantwal grid zones per event. Current validation yields 15 road routes and one secondary-distance candidate per event, with no zone lacking a delivery mode; two closure labels per event remain unresolved. The secondary option is only straight-line distance and payload-capacity context, not actual boat/drone navigation, and its zone payload needs checking. Only closures with verified geometry are applied.
- Allocation and disruption results depend on assumed inventory and demand. The near-adequate stock example is a sensitivity scenario with a 55% minimum service floor, not a real-world performance claim.
- Each event is one dated input followed by eight simulated disruption steps. This is not an operational timeline; the 2024 impact peaks are not separate model runs.
- The FastAPI backend and Module 10 dashboard are decision-support prototypes operating over generated historical files and a local SQLite database (`resq_service.sqlite3`). While authentication (RBAC for viewer and operator) and container packaging (Docker Compose) are provided, the service is designed for replay and decision-support, not real-time automated dispatch integration or multi-tenant production isolation.
- Offline frontend use is limited to read-only snapshots and checked-in local map datasets served by the static app. Live allocations, road rerouting, stock edits, and overrides need the API. The frontend is not a PWA or offline installer and references Google Fonts remotely. Docker Compose is locally verified for same-host use; it has no remote-host URL or TLS configuration, and its API health check only tests `/events`, not the generated artifacts needed by spatial operations.
- Module 10 Admin Overrides: Manual adjustments made in the allocation table are human-in-the-loop overrides, labeled strictly as "manual override, not an optimization result". Although validated by client and server against overall available inventory and a 40% donor floor, they bypass the mathematical optimization models (MILP, LinUCB, Proportional) and may lead to suboptimal distributional fairness.
- Pooled Inventory vs. Unroutable Shelters: Inventory is pooled across warehouses and shelters in the optimization core. Imposing strict per-shelter inventory and origin-destination routing constraints is counterproductive: of the 51 documented facilities in Bantwal, 16 candidate shelters lack routable road network coordinates and would artificially choke supply. In Module 10, unroutable shelters are flagged as candidate points requiring field verification before dispatch.
- Operator Stock Stale Logs: Field inventory entries recorded via the Shelter Inventory module that have not been refreshed within 24 hours carry an amber stale badge (>24h). Field stock that is unverified carries the amber `ENTERED, UNVERIFIED` status chip.
- Sourced River Gauge Telemetry: The Bantwal CWC river gauge rewind scrubber is available exclusively for the 2019-08-10 event, which contains 9 sequential documented gauge telemetry readings (peaking at 11.6 m against the 9.0 m danger mark and 8.0 m warning level). The 2024-07-31 (9.3 m peak) and 2026-08-03 (8.5 m peak) events have single documented peak levels, so intermediate rewind scrubbing is disabled for them.
- Simulation Timeline: The 8 disruption steps represent modeled escalation rounds and shortage redirects, not real-world clock time or dispatch intervals.
